## About
Hi! My name is Rizky Kusuma and I'm currently a sophomore at a senior high in Indonesia. This year, I qualified for the Indonesian Physics Olympiad (InaPhO) final round (top 60). And I hope to participate at the International Physics Olympiad (IPhO) in the near future. For the longest time, I've been interested in Physics Olympiads. Recently, however, I've been tinkering with ways to better solve Olympiad Physics problems. 
## Projects
Earlier this year I noticed how inefficient my studying method was. I spent hours working through chapters only to barely solve a third of the problems correctly. Obviously I was frustrated. But I wasn't ready to give up yet, and this led me to 'hack my way' around it, which eventually guided me to read some articles in Physics Education Research (PER). Namely, [Heller and Reif (1984)](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1207/s1532690xci0102_2) and [Chi, Feltovich, Glaser (1981)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1207/s15516709cog0502_2). 

While these articles inspired me to come up with my own problem solving approach, at first, it was still vague to me what constituted a good Olympiad Physics problem-solving approach; so I began analyzing solutions from [the toughest high-school Physics Olympiad](https://physicscup.ee/) and this project came to see the light of day.

I will be sharing and documenting my experiences with the project here.
